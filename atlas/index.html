Race Shape Atlas

How British flat races shake out at each course, trip and field size: who gets to the front, whether the draw decides it, how often early leaders last, and when winners hit the front. From Sporting Life comments on every GB flat race, Jan 2021 – Nov 2026. Wide-trip and trouble columns use Racing Post comments (2021 – Apr 2026).

How to read it
  • Early position: share of runners whose comment opens with leading, prominent (tracked/chased leaders), midfield or held up/rear.
  • Draw grip: forward rate of the low-drawn third minus the high-drawn third (races with 6+ runners), in points. On round courses stall 1 is on the inside rail, so a big positive number means a short run to the bend that hands the low draws the early pitch. On straight courses it shows which side gets forward. Place index: place rate of that draw third against the race average (100 = average).
  • Draw curve: place index (100 = race average) for stall 1, stalls 2–3, the middle, second widest and widest stall, so you can see where the draw bends, such as a crowded rail or a good widest stall. Bars above the line place better than average. The figures underneath are the share racing forward from stall 1 and the widest stall. On straight courses stall 1 is the low side, not a rail position. Small samples lean on the national curve.
  • Early leaders: horses described as leading early. "In front 2f / 1f" is read from the comment's timings; "Won" and "Placed" from the result.
  • When winners hit the front: from the winner's comment — made all or led early, took over 3f–2f out, around 1f out, or inside the final furlong.
  • Held-up horses: place rate of held-up runners as a share of forward runners' place rate (100 = they place as often). "Move late" = of held-up horses that placed, the share whose first progress came at 1f out or later.
  • Wide & trouble (Racing Post era): share of high-drawn runners described as racing wide/on the outer; share of hold-up horses with trouble in running (no room, short of room, hampered, switched): low-drawn hold-ups on round courses, where the rail is where they get boxed in; all hold-ups on straight courses.
  • Each figure is pulled towards the national average for the same race type, course type, trip and field size by an amount that depends on the sample, so small cells lean on the average. Grey "avg" figures are those national averages. Rows with fewer than 40 races are faded.
  • Race type: handicaps are about 70% of races. They are decided later and treat held-up horses better than non-handicaps, where the best horses tend to race prominently and stay there.
  • Place terms: 2 places for up to 7 runners, 3 for 8–15, 4 for 16+.
